Sheet-Metal-NC-Punch-Clamp-Avoidance-Safety-Architecture — NC Sheet Metal Punching Machine Clamp/Die Damage Prevention, Internal Cable Micro-Breakage and Clamp Warping/Datum Drift Fail-Safe, Dual-Track Failure Data Coding, and Local AI Edge Autonomous Survival Control Architecture (v1.0 Draft)
The purpose of this document is to mitigate physical interference, scraping, and collision between die surfaces and workholding clamps during X/Y table transfer in NC sheet metal punching/shearing composite machinery widely utilized across industrial sites (including manufacturer-specific designations such as ADTECH NCT series, Amada Vipros, Trumpf TruPunch, regardless of material, punching/shearing process, or control panel operating mode such as Manual, MDI, JOG, or AUTO). It aims to prevent resulting clamp/die damage, turret jamming caused by fragment penetration, clamp warping, distortion, and material datum (0-point) drift due to cumulative fatigue impact, as well as catastrophic failures arising from internal micro-breakage within sensor cable sheaths that cannot be visually identified. This system does not require complete replacement of existing machinery; rather, it deeply respects prior art patents and the domain expertise built by manufacturers and operators over time. Moving beyond the limitations of simple software keepout zones, it combines sensor redundancy and micro-conduction-based Fail-Safe mechanisms with eFPGA hardware interlocks to assist in maintaining a continuous foundation of operational safety.
Through real-time cross-validation between programmed clamp relative coordinates (commanded values) and replaceable material detection sensor outputs (actual values), cumulative fatigue warping/datum drift detection and realignment interlocks, fixed safety line (200mm fixed boundary based on standard large die station dimensions) bypass transfer logic (independent of specific G-code numbers), ultra-low latency detection of internal cable micro-breakage and intermittent open circuits with signal-loss stopping logic, standardized failure data coding with dual-track retention policies, work position re-entry control, eFPGA hardwired Safe Torque Off (STO) physical power disconnection, M80/M81 clamp lift-up integration, smart terminal mesh interoperation, and Quiet Assist with T-Reg stepped deceleration to alleviate operator fatigue and cognitive overload, this architecture adheres to soma-moa principles (Safety-II and Just Culture) by engaging hardware interlocks to safely guide machinery to a halt only when genuine risk indicators emerge.

 * Structural Limitations and Empirical Failure Mode Analysis of NC Sheet Metal Punching Machines
 * Official OSHA and ANSI B11 Classification — NC sheet metal punching machines are strictly classified under OSHA 1910.212 (General requirements for all machines), ANSI B11.0 (Safety of Machinery - General Requirements), and ANSI B11.19 (Performance Criteria for Safeguarding) as high-speed transfer equipment with hazardous points of operation. Because clamps holding sheet metal move at high speeds along X/Y tables while turret dies actuate vertically, physical interference between clamps and dies can lead to severe equipment damage and projectile/fragment hazards to personnel. (Note: Standards governing mechanical power presses, such as ANSI B11.1, ANSI B11.13, and OSHA 1910.217, explicitly exclude turret and plate punching machines and are therefore strictly excluded from the legal scope of this document.)
 * Failure Mode 1 — Collisions from Manual NC Code Insertion Errors, Manual (MDI/JOG) Operation, and Human Errors — In field operations, CAM-generated automated programs coexist with single MDI command inputs, JOG handle manual positioning, and manual G-code editing (e.g., inserting bypass codes such as G70 in ADTECH controllers). Relying on operator experience to set variable bypass distances or making visual errors during manual JOG moves can result in severe collisions where the die surface violently scrapes across the upper clamp face.
 * Failure Mode 2 — Clamp Warping, Distortion, and Material Datum Drift from Cumulative Fatigue Impact — Even when clamps do not suffer total destruction, continuous punching shockwaves and minor scraping induce cumulative mechanical fatigue, resulting in warping and distortion across clamp mounts and jaw sections. This distorts the physical datum (0-point) where material is clamped, causing dimensional errors across all punched holes and shifting the physical position outside calculated safety boundaries, which frequently triggers secondary collisions with dies.
 * Failure Mode 3 — Unstopped Servo Operation During Minor Scraping, Turret Jamming from Chipping Fragments, and Sensor Bracket Deformation — When contact between a die and clamp is a minor surface scrape rather than a total crash, X/Y servo motors may not exceed over-torque thresholds, allowing machining to continue. In such events, external replaceable sensor mounting brackets can bend, distorting target positioning and causing false readings of material presence. Furthermore, chipped fragments from damaged dies or clamp jaws can penetrate gaps beneath the lower turret disk or index bushings, leading to T-axis rotation jamming, alignment loss, and costly drive component destruction.
 * Failure Mode 4 — Internal Cable Micro-Breakage with Visually Intact Sheaths and Intermittent Ghost Signals — Sensor cables mounted on high-speed X/Y clamp tables endure continuous flexing fatigue. While the external protective sheath appears perfectly intact during visual inspection, internal copper conductors frequently suffer micro-fractures. Intermittent open circuits occur only at specific table coordinates or acceleration rates, causing the system to falsely verify material clamping or generate ghost signals that result in sudden collision hazards.
 * Failure Mode 5 — Line Stops Caused by Clamp Transfer Rail Disassembly and Repair — Following a collision, the entire clamp assembly must be removed from the transfer rail, requiring complete disassembly, replacement, and reassembly of jaws, bolts, retaining pins, and hydraulic lines, followed by origin and parallelism realignment. Line stop durations vary depending on damage severity: a minimum of 1 hour to 2 hours when replacement parts are in stock and damage is visually obvious, and up to 3 hours to 4 hours (240 minutes or more) in complex unconfirmed failures requiring diagnostic procedures to verify internal cable micro-breakage as described in Failure Mode 4.
 * Failure Mode 5-1 — Necessity of Two-Person Operation for Hydraulic Release Verification — Clamps are hydraulically locked onto transfer rails and do not automatically disengage upon pressure release due to friction and dead weight. On wide table configurations where hydraulic release foot pedals are positioned far from the clamp assembly, a single operator cannot simultaneously depress the pedal and visually confirm clamp release. Consequently, rail disassembly and reassembly typically require a two-person team (pedal operator plus clamp inspector), increasing labor allocation and overall downtime costs.
 * Failure Mode 5-2 — Double Maintenance Labor from Temporary Reassembly When Spare Parts Are Out of Stock — When replacement parts are unavailable, maintenance personnel may temporarily reassemble partially damaged components to resume urgent production, only to disassemble and reinstall genuine parts once delivered. This duplicates the rail disassembly and realignment process within a short timeframe, creating an additional layer of downtime and maintenance labor. (Note: This financial risk is treated separately from single-incident downtime calculations and is excluded from baseline efficiency multiplier formulas.)
 * Failure Mode 5-3 — Exponential Downtime Delays from External Professional AS Dispatch — When field personnel cannot resolve failures internally and must request technical service (AS) dispatch from machinery manufacturers or specialized vendors, accumulated factors—including engineer travel time, schedule queuing, diagnostic tool availability, and custom part sourcing—can escalate line stop durations to half a day (4 hours to 6 hours or more) or several days. (Note: Downtime resulting from external service reliance exceeds the internal recovery baseline of 2.4 times to 9.6 times efficiency multipliers and is categorized as an external catastrophic risk, excluded from baseline efficiency equations.)
 * Failure Mode 6 — Operator Fatigue Accumulation and Inspection Omissions During Extended Operations — During prolonged repetitive operations and night shifts, accumulating visual fatigue leads operators to omit NC code verification or misjudge clamp positioning prior to pressing cycle start, repeatedly resulting in major collision incidents.
 * Mitigation of Unintended Operational Side Effects and Modern Safety Governance — Operating under Safety-II and Just Culture frameworks, this system acts not as a surveillance tool but as a supportive framework for normal operations. Upon detecting physical collision indicators, it quietly intervenes (Quiet Assist) via eFPGA hardware interlocks and redundant sensor validation to maintain autonomous machinery survival.
 * Field Organizational, Human Factors & Machine Aging Background (Reference)
> Note: The following section provides background context regarding field operations and organizational dynamics. It does not constitute technical patent claims or primary deliverable targets of this architecture.
> 
 * AS Escalation Decision Process, Apprenticeship Learning, and Single-Operator Multi-Role Structures — In small to medium manufacturing facilities, a single operator frequently manages machine setup, program execution, and minor maintenance. While this single-operator multi-role structure fosters natural apprenticeship learning and trust building with service vendors, it also creates a structural vulnerability where operational safety depends heavily on individual skill levels. When clamp damage occurs, a senior operator first evaluates the failure to determine if internal repair is feasible. If manageable, repairs are completed internally within the 4-hour threshold. If the failure exceeds internal capabilities or occurs under less experienced personnel, the incident is escalated to management to request manufacturer AS dispatch. During service visits, junior operators observe and assist dispatch engineers during disassembly, precision alignment, and repair, acquiring field maintenance skills through informal apprenticeship. Service engineers willingly participate in this knowledge transfer because their schedules are prioritized for complex diagnostic issues (e.g., servo errors, software bugs, control wiring), whereas mechanical clamp repairs are straightforward enough to delegate to trained plant personnel. Over time, repeated service visits build mutual trust between field operators and vendor engineers, establishing a collaborative framework for resolving complex equipment issues. Concurrently, service engineers provide formal debriefings to plant managers regarding failure causes and repair details, which serve as justification for maintenance billing. (Note: This administrative explanation and billing process constitutes a separate organizational layer and is excluded from technical downtime equations covering 1 hour to 4 hours or 2.4 times to 9.6 times efficiency calculations.)
 * Machine Aging and Long-Term Ownership Failure Mode Intensification Trends — The occurrence frequency and probability of Failure Modes 1 through 6 increase in direct proportion to machine age and operating hours (without altering the single-incident repair baseline duration of 1 hour to 4 hours). Aging machinery experiences higher rates of clamp warping (Failure Mode 2) and cable micro-breakage (Failure Mode 4) due to cumulative stress, while obsolete component availability increases the likelihood of temporary reassembly (Failure Mode 5-2). Conversely, long-term ownership fosters cumulative field experience and vendor familiarity, enabling service engineers to readily identify machine-specific recurring failure patterns during dispatches.
 * 200mm Fixed Safety Line, Sensor Redundancy Fail-Safe, and eFPGA Physical Disconnection Mechanism
 * 200mm Fixed Safety Line Avoidance Logic — Among standard turret die station sizes ranging from 12.7mm to 114.3mm (large station category), avoiding physical contact with the maximum die diameter (114.3mm) while accounting for rotational inertia, machine vibration, and thermal expansion requires a minimum clearance of 150mm. Establishing a fixed safety line at 200mm provides a universal buffer independent of specific manufacturer station layouts. Implementing a single conditional check — if actual clamp Y < 200 then issue bypass transfer command (e.g., ADTECH series G70 Y200) — systematically mitigates die interference across all turret stations.
 * Commanded vs. Actual Sensor Dual-Track and 0-Point Realignment Fail-Safe System — Programmed relative clamp positions (commanded values) are continuously cross-checked against external replaceable material detection sensors (actual values). Subsequent axis motion commands (e.g., G01) execute only when both values match within defined tolerances.
   * Mode Invariance and Datum Drift Defense — Across both Manual (JOG/MDI) and Automatic (AUTO) modes, if operator positioning errors or cumulative fatigue impacts cause clamp warping and shift the material datum (0-point) beyond allowable thresholds, the system detects the deviation, prompts a machine stop, and triggers zero-point alignment interlocks.
   * Internal Cable Micro-Breakage and Dynamic Impedance Detection (Internal Micro-Breakage Fail-Safe) — To detect conductor micro-fractures and intermittent opens undetectable by visual inspection, an eFPGA-based circuit performs real-time dynamic conduction and micro-impedance (resistance variation) monitoring. Upon detecting sub-1ms signal dropouts or impedance noise during high-speed table movement, the system triggers an immediate NC Fail-Safe stop without software latency, preventing secondary collisions.
 * eFPGA Hardwired STO Physical Disconnection Integration — Upon detecting sensor mismatch, datum shift, bracket deformation, or internal wire breakage, the eFPGA hardware circuit bypasses software processing to directly open the motor driver's Safe Torque Off (STO) circuit, achieving physical power disconnection and preventing turret jamming.
 * Time Loss and Defense Efficiency Analysis — Applying a 200mm fixed safety line adds approximately 5 seconds of bypass motion per sheet. For a daily throughput of 300 sheets, total accumulated motion delay equals 1,500 seconds (25 minutes per day). In contrast, a single clamp collision causes line stop durations ranging from a minimum of 1 hour to 2 hours (in-stock parts, obvious damage) up to 3 hours to 4 hours (240 minutes, complex unconfirmed failures). Preventing one collision yields a time preservation ratio of approximately 2.4 times under minimum downtime conditions and up to 9.6 times under maximum downtime conditions, demonstrating clear engineering efficiency by investing 25 minutes daily to prevent 1 hour to 4 hours of line stoppage.
 * NC Code Implementation Example and Controller Designation Invariance — Bypass commands inserted into CAM-generated NC files (e.g., G70 in ADTECH controllers) represent controller-specific syntax examples rather than fixed international standards. The core innovation lies in the underlying logic structure — executing bypass transfer to a fixed safety line without punching commands before re-entering the work area — which can be substituted with equivalent rapid positioning commands across any CNC controller brand.
   * Modified Code Example:
     N110 G70 Y200. (Example: ADTECH non-punching rapid positioning G-code to fixed safety line; equivalent commands apply for other controllers)
     N111 G00 X100. Y10. (Safe re-entry to target work coordinates)
     N112 G28 P... (Execute punching operation)
 * Specialized Sensor Expansion, Smart Terminal Mesh Infrastructure, and Local AI Reserved Architecture
 * Auxiliary Sensor Expansion Slots — Beyond baseline positioning sensors, dedicated sensor input channels are reserved at edge nodes to evaluate high-speed X/Y transfer, punching vibration, datum shift, and cable integrity.
 * Material Datum Shift and Cable Conductor Diagnostic Module — Evaluates clamp warping/datum shifts and cable flex fatigue prior to cycle execution, issuing preventive alerts.
 * Abnormal Vibration and Chipping Fragment Impact Sensor — Utilizes high-frequency acoustic/vibration sensors to analyze punching shockwaves, X/Y motion spectra, and high-frequency friction noise caused by detached die or jaw fragments.
 * Smart Terminal Mesh Communications Infrastructure — Establishes mesh communication across security watches, smartphones, industrial PDAs, and machine smart tags to cross-validate operator proximity and setup configurations in real time.
 * Local Vision AI Detection Agent — Incorporates expandable lightweight vision models to inspect sheet positioning, clamp jaw distortion, turret disk gaps, and operator hand intrusion on a frame-by-frame basis.
 * Ultra-Low Latency Control, Dual-Track Data Retention, and T-Reg Governance
 * eFPGA-Based Hardware Interlock Control — Bypassing software layers, hardware logic (eFPGA) initiates motor STO cutoff within a target response time of under 1ms upon detecting sensor mismatch, datum drift, internal cable micro-breakage, or clamp interference across all operating modes.
 * T-Reg Stepped Deceleration Mode — Upon receiving alerts regarding signal instability, minor datum shifts, impedance noise, or operator fatigue indicators, the system progressively reduces X/Y transfer velocities (e.g., 15% reduction or n% variable deceleration) to ensure cushioned stopping.
 * Dual-Track Data Retention Policy for International Safety Compliance — To satisfy ISO 45001 and KOSHA-MS management guidelines, data processing is split into two independent streams.
   * Track 1 (Privacy-Preserved Real-Time Control Track) — Raw vision and audio streams are anonymized at the edge node and permanently purged within 10 seconds.
   * Track 2 (Safety Telemetry and Failure Data Code Long-Term Retention Track) — Operational logs detailing clamp collisions, sensor mismatches, datum shifts, cable micro-breakages, minor scrapes, near-misses, and machine faults are converted into standardized Failure Data Codes and stored as encrypted ledgers (CBOR/Ledger). These codes serve as foundational training assets to refine edge rule-based algorithms, retrain AI models, and advance predictive maintenance capabilities.

 * Generic Concept Invariance and Scope Definitions
 * Avoidance Distance and Numerical Value Invariance — Avoidance parameters (e.g., 150mm, 200mm) serve as illustrative engineering examples. Any safety distance exceeding maximum die dimensions to prevent clamp collision falls within the scope of this prior art disclosure. All bypass transfer routines setting safety boundaries equal to or greater than these values are considered equivalent structures.
 * Material and Thickness Invariance — The term "sheet metal" encompasses cold-rolled steel (CR), stainless steel (SUS), aluminum (AL), galvanized steel (GI), copper/brass, and all thin metallic sheets regardless of alloy composition or thickness.
 * Control Panel Operating Mode and Operator Working Structure Invariance — Safety interlocks apply universally across manual modes (JOG, MDI, setup) and automated modes (AUTO, automated loader integration), as well as across single-operator multi-role setups and dedicated maintenance team environments.
 * NC Control Code (G/M Code) Designation Invariance — References to code G70 represent controller-specific examples (e.g., ADTECH). All G/M codes, macros, subroutines, or controller instructions executing non-punching transfer to a fixed safety line are functionally equivalent.
 * Failure Data Coding and Dual-Track Storage Invariance — Structure and processing routines that convert fault logs, minor scrapes, and near-misses into standardized failure data codes stored in secondary locations for algorithm refinement fall fully within this disclosure.
 * Protocol, Sensor/Terminal Combination (n≥1), Relative Kinematics, and Scale Invariance — Applies regardless of communication protocols, sensor counts/types (n≥1), smart terminal configurations, machine scale, or relative motion kinematics (moving tool, moving workpiece, or combined motion).
