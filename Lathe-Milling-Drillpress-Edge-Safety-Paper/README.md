Lathe-Milling-Drillpress-Edge-Safety-Paper — Stationary Rotating Machinery (Lathe, Milling, Drill Press) Flying Object/Entanglement Mitigation and Local AI Edge Autonomous Survival Control Architecture (v1.0 Core Baseline)
> The purpose of this document is to mitigate high-speed flying object (projectile) and entanglement (jamming/catching) hazards occurring in stationary rotating machinery commonly used in industrial fields, such as lathes, milling machines, and drill presses. Similar to the Press/Press Brake architecture, this system does not demand the complete replacement of existing equipment. It deeply respects the prior art patents and unique craftsmanship accumulated by manufacturers and operators over a long period. Its primary goal is not to monitor workers, but to assist in establishing a continuous safety foundation.
> By utilizing abnormal vibration pre-detection for lathe chuck loosening, Dynamic Hazard Zone Tracking synced with milling spindle coordinate movements, drill press material clamping verification and anti-stall interlock, and Quiet Assist control during cognitive judgment degradation due to cumulative fatigue, the system aims to alleviate operational fatigue. Governed by the soma-moa philosophy (Safety-II and Just Culture), it aims to safely cut off machine drive via eFPGA 0.1ms ultra-low latency intervention only when imminent hazard symptoms manifest.

1. Structural Limits and Frequent Empirical Failure Modes of Lathe, Milling, and Drill Press Hazards
 * OSHA and ANSI B11 Official Classification — Lathes, Milling Machines, and Drill Presses, alongside presses, are strictly classified as machinery with rotating Points of Operation under OSHA 1910.212 and the ANSI B11 series (B11.6 Manual Turning Machines, B11.8 Manual Milling/Drilling/Boring Machines). While the primary hazard of presses is crushing via vertical motion, the core hazard typologies for these rotating machines manifest as detachment, flying objects (projectiles), and entanglement.
 * Actual Failure Mode 1 — Lathe Chuck Loosening and Material Defect-Induced Flying Objects — According to the Korea Occupational Safety and Health Agency (KOSHA) statistics and case studies on machining accidents, a significant portion of severe accidents during manual lathe operations are identified as flying object hazards caused by rotating body detachment. In an open structure between the chuck and the tool post, the loosening of chuck jaw gripping force due to repeated vibration, insufficient tailstock securing, or material fracture during cutting due to internal voids/irregularities acts as the main cause. This consistently leads to a fatal pattern where high-speed rotating heavy objects strike the operator.
 * Actual Failure Mode 2 — Milling Multi-Axis Coordinate Movement and Tool Collision Hazards — Analyzing accident patterns in milling processes reveals that, unlike presses with fixed hazard points, accidents frequently occur due to the structural blind spot where the hazard zone dynamically moves along the X/Y/Z 3-axes every second. Tool breakage occurs when the spindle approaches the workpiece, and projectile accidents from metal chips due to poor clamp fixing are frequent. Due to the fluid nature of the hazard zone, simple fixed optical sensors or cover-type interlocks inevitably create monitoring blind spots.
 * Actual Failure Mode 3 — Drill Press Material Unclamping, Hole Cutter Jamming (Stall), and Entanglement/Flying Object Hazards from Forced Manual Removal Attempts — Statistically, major severe accidents in drill press operations frequently occur due to poor material clamping and tool stalling during machining. When machining thin plates or pipes, if a hole cutter or drill bit instantaneously jams (stalls) inside the material due to chip accumulation, excessive feed, or lack of cutting fluid, operators often attempt the dangerous practice of forcefully pulling out the material or tool using the handle lever or bare hands while the machine power is still on and rotational drive is actively applied. This reaction to the accumulated over-torque in the spindle causes the material to violently spin along with the vise, shatters the hole cutter blades into flying projectiles, or instantly drags the operator's cotton gloves and body into the rotating axis, leading to a chain of fatal injuries, as consistently warned by KOSHA average accident reports.
 * Actual Failure Mode 4 — Concentration/Judgment Degradation due to Prolonged Continuous Work and Cumulative Fatigue — According to KOSHA and Human Factors research data, visual and physical fatigue accumulates during shift work, night machining, and prolonged repetitive tasks, leading to a significantly delayed cognitive response pattern. When attention drops due to fatigue, operators may unconsciously reach out to clear chips while the spindle is still spinning, or mistake a rotating body for being stationary due to a stroboscopic effect, frequently resulting in massive entanglement accidents directly caused by a lack of judgment.
 * Mitigation of Potential Operational Adverse Effects and Application of Modern Safety Governance — This system is not a surveillance tool intended to control or restrict workers. Inheriting the tone of the Press White Paper, it flexibly supplements 9,999 normal operations under the principles of Safety-II and Just Culture. It aims to perform autonomous survival control by quietly intervening (Quiet Assist) only when detecting hazard zone intrusion, poor clamping, or abnormal physical postures caused by fatigue.
2. Dynamic Hazard Zone Tracking, Abnormal Vibration Detection, Clamping/Stall Interlock, and Fatigue State Assistance Mechanisms
 * Lathe Assistance: Abnormal Vibration and Eccentricity Detection-Based Flying Object Prediction — To proactively defend against projectile fractures caused by lathe chuck loosening or material defects, logic is applied to analyze micro-changes in the vibration spectrum around the chuck and tailstock in real-time. Immediately upon detecting abnormal vibration symptoms outside normal rotational frequencies or predicting a projectile trajectory, a PRELOCK is triggered to induce motor power cutoff. (Specific RPM thresholds and vibration spectrum analysis parameters remain in the trade secret domain for field calibration and PoC phases.)
 * Milling Assistance: Coordinate-Linked Dynamic Hazard Zone Tracking — To overcome the monitoring limitations of fixed sensors, complex fusion sensors receive the real-time coordinates (X/Y/Z) of the equipment spindle and control the hazard zone itself to dynamically track and move along the spindle's trajectory. If the relative distance between the spindle and the human body breaches the safety clearance, it immediately induces deceleration and stopping to mitigate collisions between the machine and the human body.
 * Drill Press Assistance: Material Clamping Verification and Hole Cutter/Bit Stall Instant Over-Torque Automatic Cutoff Interlock (Material Clamping & Anti-Stall Interlock) — Alongside the material clamping verification interlock, this mechanism detects abnormal symptoms, such as the spindle stalling or current/torque spikes occurring in 0.1ms increments, when a hole cutter or drill bit jams into the material during machining. Before the operator acts on the dangerous impulse to forcefully extract the material or tool while the rotational drive is still active, the eFPGA hardware aims to automatically cut off motor power immediately upon detecting over-torque. It disallows reactivation until main power cutoff is verified, proactively mitigating rebound shocks, tool fracture projectiles, and entanglement accidents.
 * Worker Fatigue Accumulation Assistance: Security Watch Haptic Alerts and T-Reg Performance Degradation Linkage — To prevent accidents caused by operators' impaired judgment, non-invasive multimodal sensing is combined with Quiet Assist haptic notifications. When signs of drowsiness or abnormal operational postures are detected due to prolonged work, individual security watch haptic vibrations (rather than loud public alarms) provide preemptive warnings. Upon reaching the fatigue threshold, the T-Reg 15% performance degradation mode is activated, decelerating the machine's operating speed to physically mitigate exposure to hazards resulting from sudden judgment lapses.
 * Common Edge Control and Immediate Recovery Mechanism Mutual Citation — All hazard signals captured from the aforementioned machinery are processed without passing through external networks, mutually citing the identical 'eFPGA-based 0.1ms ultra-low latency motor stop control' and 'immediate safe position recovery' logic established in Chapter 4 of the Press White Paper. Through this, kinetic energy is dissipated in the shortest possible time during an accident to prevent secondary injuries.
3. Specialized Sensor Extension Arrays for Rotating Machinery and Local AI Analysis Reserved Areas
 * Definition of Additional Sensor Extension Slots — Beyond the basic triple sensors (FMCW Radar, Thermal Imaging, Capacitive), dedicated sensor addition spaces are reserved in the modular edge node to respond to hazard types unique to rotating machinery.
 * Abnormal Acoustic/Vibration Microphone Sensor Module — Encompasses the addition of sensors that analyze in real-time the high-frequency fracture sounds and cutting noise spectrum changes that occur during tool wear, chip jamming, hole cutter stalling, or cutting bite cracks.
 * Ultrasonic and Laser Distance Displacement Sensor Extension — Secures micro-displacement sensor mounting channels for real-time tracking of workpiece eccentricity and tool wear axes.
 * Local Vision AI Entanglement Symptom Detection Agent — Encompasses the reserved expansion space for ultra-lightweight Vision-Language/CNN agents that analyze, frame-by-frame, the pattern of a worker's gloves, clothes, or workwear sleeves approaching the hazardous clearance near the rotating axis.
4. Overcoming Physical Constraints, Ultra-Low Latency Motor Stop, and T-Reg Governance
 * eFPGA-Based 0.1ms Ultra-Low Latency Control Mutual Citation — Applying the hardware interlock control system from Chapter 4 of the Press White Paper, this induces spindle motor power cutoff at an execution speed of the 0.1ms level without software OS thread delays when rotational abnormalities, tool stalling, or body intrusions are detected.
 * T-Reg 15% Fail-PRELOCK Mode — When an intrusion into the sensor detection boundary zone occurs, material clamping instability is warned, or the worker fatigue accumulation mode is activated, machining output is degraded to a 15% level to mitigate accident injuries caused by sudden rotational force.
 * Anonymous Logging and PII 10-Second Destruction — Complying with the soma-moa Just Culture principles, human surveillance noise is excluded. Only anonymized CBOR physical delta logs are retained, and raw video/audio data is permanently destroyed from volatile memory within 10 seconds.

 * Small/Large Drill Press Line Application — Vise/clamp fastening verification and spindle stall current sensing terminals are directly and non-invasively connected to the motor control circuit to assist in cutting off drive when material is unfixed or when stalling occurs.
